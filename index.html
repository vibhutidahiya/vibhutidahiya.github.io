Work Highlights

Five pieces of work that show how I operate, from the first assessment to a plan a team can act on.

A Common Controls Framework, built into an AI-enabled GRC platform

Accorian

The situation

Healthcare and SaaS clients were auditing against ISO 27001, SOC 2, NIST CSF, and HIPAA separately, and Accorian needed a way to assess all of them consistently through its AI-enabled GRC platform.

What I did

I built a Common Controls Framework unifying ISO 27001, SOC 2 (all five Trust Services Criteria), NIST CSF 2.0, and HIPAA into a single control set, then authored the assessment questionnaire for Accorian's AI-enabled GRC platform, GORICO — questions across 27 domains spanning Governance, Legal & Privacy, IT-InfoSec, Third-Party Management, HR, and Physical Security, each mapped back to a CCF control.

What it shows

I can turn a framework into something a platform can actually run on, not just a document.

A first SOC 2 gap assessment for a biotechnology research organization

Accorian

The situation

A biotechnology research organization was working toward its first SOC 2 attestation and needed to know where its controls stood.

What I did

I led the gap assessment across all five Trust Services Criteria — Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, and Privacy — and built a detailed remediation roadmap to attestation readiness.

What it shows

I can take a team from not knowing where it stands to a clear plan for reaching attestation readiness.

Risk assessments and data localization reviews for India's largest credit bureau

Grant Thornton Bharat

The situation

India's largest Credit Information Company needed its partners assessed against RBI guidelines, and needed to know which of its products were storing data outside India in violation of those same requirements.

What I did

I delivered risk assessments for 25+ new and existing partners, evaluating IT environments, security posture, and data handling before approval, and separately conducted data localization reviews across the credit bureau's products — mapping data flows through stakeholder interviews and product demos to flag violations and deliver a risk-rated, application-level remediation plan.

What it shows

I can hold a consistent standard across many partners while still digging into product-level detail where it matters.

CIS Benchmark-aligned Secure Configuration Documents across 70+ technologies

Grant Thornton Bharat

The situation

Two clients — India's largest Credit Information Company and a large public sector bank — each needed a consistent, auditable security baseline across more than 70 technologies, including Windows and Unix operating systems, web and database servers, and virtualization software.

What I did

I developed CIS Benchmark-aligned Secure Configuration Documents and automated review scripts in separate engagements for the credit bureau and the bank. For the bank, I went further, performing periodic configuration reviews against the SCDs and reviews of the automated scripts themselves.

What it shows

I can build a reusable security baseline once and apply it responsibly across more than one client, not just hand off a document and move on.

Turning data sharing agreements into software requirements

Georgia Institute of Technology

The situation

Data Sharing Agreements hold privacy and security obligations in dense legal language.

What I did

As part of SafeInsights, an NSF-funded (National Science Foundation) project, I designed the methodology and engineered generative AI prompt strategies to extract, classify, and structure software, privacy, and security requirements from real-world Data Sharing Agreements.

What it shows

Legal obligations can become structured software artifacts that support privacy-by-design and secure-by-design implementation.
